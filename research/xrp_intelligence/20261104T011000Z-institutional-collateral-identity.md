## Highest-value findings

### Coinbase's Q3 filing is more informative than the headline metrics

Coinbase's Q3 2026 10-Q says its 2026 priorities are the everything exchange, stablecoins/payments and onchain adoption. It says the Circle arrangement was renewed on the same terms. Q3 transaction revenue fell 44% year over year; consumer spot volume fell 59% and institutional spot volume fell 53%, while derivatives and prediction markets offset part of the decline.

Q3 stablecoin revenue was $306.9M versus $324.5M a year earlier. Coinbase says higher average USDC balances contributed $84.6M, offset by $52.8M from lower average rates. Average USDC held in Coinbase products was reported at $22B, up 49% year over year.

The Q3 filing reports $1.976B total loan receivables, of which $1.959B were fiat/payment-stablecoin receivables and $17.6M were crypto-asset receivables. Recognized crypto lending collateral was $997.6M: BTC $920.6M, ETH $67.0M and other crypto assets $9.9M. Recognized derivatives collateral was $301.9M and the disclosed table lists BTC and ETH, not XRP.

This does **not** disprove Coinbase Global Exchange product-level XRP collateral eligibility. It does mean the public Q3 filing does not demonstrate material XRP collateral utilization. The entire recognized lending-collateral "other crypto assets" aggregate is $9.9M; if XRP is included in it, it cannot exceed that aggregate at the reporting date. The filing's separate $3.285B borrower-pledged collateral table is explicitly not recognized by Coinbase as its own collateral; do not treat it as Coinbase-owned or XRP utilization.

Primary source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1679788/000167978826000122/coin-20260930.htm

### Circle's economics with Coinbase

Circle's Q2 2026 10-Q says its Coinbase collaboration agreement pays allocations based on USDC held on Coinbase after issuer retention, plus half of certain remaining ecosystem-growth amounts after approved third-party participant payments. Circle reported $324.6M of distribution costs tied to Coinbase in Q2 and $655.3M for H1 2026.

Coinbase's Q3 10-Q says the agreement was renewed on the same terms. These are distribution costs and contractual economics, not Coinbase net profit and not XRP capital flows.

### Stablecoins and XRPL: direct network integration, not proof of XRP demand

Circle says USDC is native on XRPL, requires no third-party bridge, and is supported by Circle Mint and APIs. Qualified businesses can convert fiat directly into XRPL USDC and redeem 1:1 for USD.

Coinbase launched RLUSD on Prime, Exchange and retail on October 28, 2026, with deposits/withdrawals across XRPL, Base and Ethereum. Its announcement says Global Exchange collateral use would follow; it was not available at launch. Ripple said it would provide Base liquidity.

These relationships establish stablecoin distribution on XRPL. They do not prove that the stablecoin transfers use XRP as the bridge asset or generate incremental XRP purchases.

### XRP collateral: distinguish venue eligibility from a regulator-wide approval

Bitnomial announced XRP and RLUSD margin collateral for its derivatives complex, subject to applicable regulatory approvals. Its current clearinghouse schedule lists XRP with a 40% haircut and a $15M notional limit. This is a venue-specific product schedule, not universal CFTC treatment.

The CFTC's December 8, 2025 digital-assets pilot initially limited the first three months of FCM reliance on its no-action position to BTC, ETH and USDC. XRP was not on that initial list. The CFTC release separately discusses tokenized real-world assets such as Treasuries and money-market funds.

The strongest defensible statement is: **Bitnomial lists XRP as venue-specific margin collateral under its product framework; the reviewed CFTC pilot did not grant blanket XRP eligibility.** Actual posted XRP and utilization are unknown.

## Competing hypotheses

1. **Stablecoin complement:** USDC/RLUSD expand XRPL activity while XRP remains useful in some bridge/liquidity routes.
2. **Stablecoin substitution:** stablecoins settle dollar legs directly and reduce XRP bridge demand on some routes.
3. **Collateral capital efficiency:** XRP collateral eligibility makes existing holdings more productive and can reduce forced spot sales.
4. **Collateral reflexivity:** leveraged collateral can amplify selloffs through margin calls and liquidation.
5. **Coinbase platform diversification:** Coinbase is scaling exchange, custody, derivatives, stablecoins, payments and onchain services; XRP is one asset within the platform.
6. **Adoption is not price:** stablecoin and XRPL adoption can increase without proportional XRP price appreciation.

No hypothesis is accepted as causal until route-level flows, collateral utilization and out-of-sample price response are measured.
