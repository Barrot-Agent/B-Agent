# How to Use This Guide

There are three ways in. Pick one and finish it before you start another.

**If you are the operator.** Read Part One, then Chapter 13, then Part Five. You
are deciding whether to replace a line item in your own business. The decision
turns on two numbers — the fully loaded cost of the job today, and the error rate
you can tolerate — and those are Chapters 4 and 28.

**If you are the builder.** Read Part Two with the vendor documentation open in a
second window, then build the employee in Part Three. Do not skip Chapter 12. The
fence is not a compliance ornament; it is the reason you can leave the system
alone at night.

**If you are the seller.** Read Part Four first, then come back to Part Three so
you know what you are actually promising. Selling a "bot" is a losing pitch. The
pitch that closes is a finished job with a delivery date.

Three rules of use:

1. **Build one employee end to end before reading Part Four.** Reading about
   pricing before you have a deliverable produces confident nonsense.
2. **Keep a file called `done.md`.** Every job you automate gets a written
   definition of done, a sample of good output, and a sample of bad output. If you
   cannot write it, you cannot delegate it.
3. **Run the numbers with your own inputs.** Every table in this book has a
   formula behind it. Copy the formula, replace the assumptions, and see whether
   the business still works at half the price and twice the error rate. If it only
   works at the optimistic point, it does not work.

Notation used throughout: **L1–L6** refer to the six layers of the GrokBot OS;
**$XM** means $X thousand per month; "page" means a 320-word book page, and every
page count in this book is measured from the manuscript, not typed by hand.
